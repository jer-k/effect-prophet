# Development search and final holdout

**Stage H implementation note.** Per the revised EP-089 scope, this written workflow and interpretation guide replaces the proposed built-package walkthrough and independent consumer run. It is not evidence that anyone performed such a run. Reports are experimental; a report's local schema version is not a cross-release migration promise.

## Partition the data before looking at holdout outcomes

Choose a cutoff _before_ configuration search. Complete rows at or before the cutoff form **development** history; later complete rows form the **final holdout**. All rows within each partition must be sorted by strictly increasing canonical UTC timestamps; the first holdout timestamp must be later than the last development timestamp. Supply actual targets for holdout evaluation, and all known future regressors, conditions and bounds required by the selected model. The library does not clean data, fill missing targets, infer calendars, or guarantee that a human has not looked at the holdout.

Within development, `searchModels` fits candidates repeatedly on earlier rolling-origin training prefixes and scores their predictions against later **development** folds. It chooses the minimum declared overall point-error objective; lower scores are selection evidence, not an unbiased estimate of future quality. Candidates use identical folds. Optional intervals never become a hidden ranking criterion. Decide candidates, folds, objective, zero-actual policy, baselines, holdout partition and interval controls before seeing final results.

After selecting, read `search.candidates[search.selected.candidateIndex]`, branch on `candidate?.kind === "success"` (otherwise handle the inconsistency as a caller error), and construct a receipt from its `id`, `candidateIndex`, _complete encoded_ `options`, and `score` without a cast. Pass both the `ModelSearchResult` and this receipt to `evaluateHoldout`, along with the original development and later holdout rows. As with search, the returned Effect requires a `FittingBackend` Layer and exposes typed validation, metric and holdout failures; pure report encoding/decoding require no Layer. The operation verifies the receipt against search, fits the selected options **once on all development rows**, projects holdout rows without target values into point prediction, optionally simulates seeded MAP intervals, then calculates overall holdout metrics and train-only baselines on the same holdout instants. It does not rerun search, refit on holdout, or return a fitted handle. Receipt checks establish internal consistency, not cryptographic provenance. A caller who repeatedly checks holdout results cannot call the last run a one-time assessment.

The `metrics` policy must use overall aggregation, include the selection objective, and retain its exact-zero policy. Coverage requires interval mode; baselines omit coverage because they have no predictive intervals. A baseline such as seasonal-naive uses only _exact_ development timestamps at the requested lag; if even one holdout timestamp has no training match, the operation fails rather than silently comparing different rows. As with every metric, units matter: MAE/RMSE are in target units, MSE in squared units, and MAPE/MDAPE/sMAPE are ratios. Coverage is a fraction of actual targets inside value bounds; it is not a universal calibration guarantee or a significance test. Side-by-side baselines are not skill ratios or a claim of superiority.

The caller supplies bounded printable `provenance.build` and `provenance.environment` labels; do not put credentials in them. The experimental report contains partition sizes/ranges, development search plan/candidate outcomes and score, selected options, fitted model kind and a safe MAP fit-summary projection, forecasts (including actual business values), shared metric policy and baseline forecasts/scores. It excludes training rows, model handles, raw WASM buffers, simulation state, error causes and stacks. Report bodies are **caller-controlled potentially sensitive data**: store, redact, retain or delete them under the application's policy. The library does not write files or databases; JSON stringify/parse and `encodeEvaluationReport`/`decodeEvaluationReport` provide an I/O-free strict roundtrip. Decoding never fits or loads WASM. Never log report contents or put timestamps, candidate IDs, scores, targets, options or seeds in tracing attributes.

## Interpretation and limitations

This document, rather than a built-package consumer run, is the EP-089 explanation deliverable. Development-fold scores compare configurations; one final holdout score evaluates the already-selected configuration on later data. These are not exchangeable estimates: optimizing on folds makes the development score optimistic, and inspecting or tuning on the final holdout repeatedly invalidates its one-time interpretation. Intervals measure conditional predictive uncertainty under the implemented MAP simulation model; empirical coverage on a small holdout neither establishes universal calibration nor proves a configuration is better. Baseline scores are same-row context only, not statistical significance. No independent run, reviewer identity or review findings are claimed.
